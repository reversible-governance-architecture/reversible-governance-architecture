# **Appendix A — Implementation Sketches**

These sketches illustrate potential implementations of the design constraints.

They are not models.
They are not recommendations.
They do not depict complete systems.

Each sketch is partial.
Each is replaceable.

---

## **A.1 — Distributed Review Cells (DRC)**

*Operationalizes: Scan Mechanism (4.3) and Graduated Escalation (4.4)*

The scan mechanism requires epistemic redundancy without a permanent institution.

Distributed Review Cells are one possible form:
temporary, independent units that generate structural risk analyses but do not make decisions.

**Access and Selection:**
Open access, subject to qualification that assesses systemic thinking rather than ideology or affiliation.
Selection is by lottery from a qualified pool, with limited terms and no immediate reappointment.
This reduces self-perpetuation and lobbying.

**Incentive Structure:**
Participation is possible without a career path or institutional power accumulation.
Compensation and specific arrangements are context-dependent. The crucial factor is that incentives are not geared toward power accumulation.

**Redundancy:**
Multiple cells work independently and in parallel on the same topic.
Divergences are made visible, not smoothed over.
Escalation is triggered not by individual analyses, but by convergence across multiple independent perspectives.

**Output:**
Structural risk analyses, reversibility metrics, and dependency mapping.
The impact is achieved exclusively through the escalation mechanism, not through direct intervention.

**Self-limitation:**
The DRC structure itself is also subject to rotation, evaluation, and sunset logic.

---

**Limitations of this form:**
The calibration of access and qualification remains an open issue.
Thresholds that are too low generate noise, and thresholds that are too high create gatekeeping.
Strategic subversion cannot be ruled out.

Design cannot prevent manipulation; it can only make it more costly and visible.

Fundamentally:
No system can eliminate bribery.
The question is not whether it will happen, but rather how costly and visible it will be.

## **A.2 — Epistemic Track Record**

*Operationalizes: Leverage metric (4.2) and transparency as an adaptability mechanism*

High-leverage decisions are based on implicit assumptions about impact, duration, side effects, and uncertainty.

In many systems, these assumptions disappear once the decision is made.

The Epistemic Track Record is one way to keep these assumptions visible.

**Basic Logic:**
Decisions above a defined leverage threshold are accompanied by documented assumptions, expected effects, and a time horizon.

The evolution of these assumptions is later assessed.

Not in the sense of “right or wrong,”
but in the sense of:

Were the assumptions explicit?
Were uncertainties identified?
Were adjustments made in light of new information?

**Function:**
The track record makes epistemic practice visible.
It does not alter access or power relations.
It shows how decisions were conceived and how they have behaved over time.

---

**Limitations of this approach:**
Any form of measurability leads to adaptation.
Actors may learn to favor certain predictions or strategically frame uncertainty.
The balance between meaningfulness and playability remains uncertain.

Fundamentally:
A visibility mechanism only works if it is used.
If no one looks, formal compliance arises instead of the capacity to learn.

## **A.3 — Representation and Problem Solving**

*Instantiated: Structural Resilience (Axis II) and Self-Limitation of Power*

In many coordination systems, the functions of legitimacy and problem-solving are structurally combined.

The same bodies communicate externally, make decisions, and are expected to conduct complex analysis and evaluate long-term effects.

These requirements are structurally in tension.
Visibility is rewarded more quickly than quality of thought.

Systems often informally resolve this tension through consultants, expert committees, or outsourced analysis.
However, these structures are often opaque and irreversible.

RGA explicitly outlines a possible separation:

- Mandate-bearing level: Legitimacy, decision-making, and responsibility
- Analytical level: Modeling, evaluation, and understanding of structure

The separation is functional, not hierarchical.

Analysis informs. Decision remains bound.

---

**Limits of this form:**
The boundary between the two functions is unstable.
Defining it is a matter of power itself.
Visibility does not protect against instrumentalization.
It merely makes it recognizable.

Fundamentally:
If analysis is visible but not bound by a mandate, it is unclear whether responsibility is actually assumed.

Whether this separation is perceived as a relief or a disempowerment is not a design question.
